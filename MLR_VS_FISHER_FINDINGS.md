# MLR vs Fisher Information: Empirical Head-to-Head on H2/H2+

> **STATUS (2026-04-18, superseded):** The H2 result below is an **in-sample best fit on 7–8 radii** and **did not generalize**: on G2-1 LOOCV (12 molecules, held-out), Fisher beat MLR 12/12 with ΔBIC = −20.3 (very strong evidence for Fisher). The experiment used λ_SCC (a dimensionless implementation rescaling) as a stand-in for MLR's λ_E with no derivation linking the two, so this was **not a test of MLR theory**. See the "2026-04-18 update" section below. The sections between here and that update are retained for audit trail only and should not be quoted.

**Date:** 2026-04-18
**Scripts:** `mlr_vs_fisher_ablation.py`, `mlr_vs_fisher_ablation_v2.py`, `mlr_vs_fisher_calibrated.py`
**Data:** `composability_h2.csv`, `composability_h2plus.csv`, `ablation_v2_*.csv`, `calibrated_v2_results.csv`

## Question

Does Miller's Law of Representation (MLR), with its explicit energy term E, fit
DFT correction data better than Frieden's Fisher Information Field Theory (which
has no energy term)? The structural difference between the two frameworks is the
E/(k_B T ln 2) term in MLR. If ablating that term degrades fit quality more than
its single extra parameter justifies (BIC), the energy term is doing real work.

In curv-dfa terms:
- Fisher (K+H) — `FisherModule` with harmonizer + core-suppression gate
- E term — `SCCModule` (Self-Coupling Cancellation v2 with density gate)
- MLR (K+H+E) — both modules composed

## Three runs, increasing rigor

### v1 — naive ablation on existing CSVs
**Files:** `mlr_vs_fisher_ablation.py`, reading `composability_h2.csv` / `composability_h2plus.csv`
**Result:** Fisher dominates (ΔBIC = -24 H2, -14 H2+)

**Two methodological flaws found on review:**

1. **Wrong reference**: HF/UHF used as "truth" but HF lacks correlation energy.
   For H2, "distance to HF" rewards methods that strip out correlation —
   exactly the wrong incentive.
2. **Wrong SCC implementation**: The CSV files were generated by
   `composability_test.py` using the *inline* SCC v1 (no density-suppression
   gate). The current canonical SCC v2 lives in `rift_functionals.py` and adds
   `h(ρ) = 1/(1+(ρ/ρ_s)^p)` specifically because v1 over-corrects in
   many-electron regions. v1 was retired for cause; we shouldn't be testing MLR
   against an implementation that's already been superseded.

### v2 — proper SCC v2 + correlated reference
**File:** `mlr_vs_fisher_ablation_v2.py`
**Reference:** UHF for H2+ (exact at dissociation), CCSD(T) for H2
**Calibration:** defaults from `experiments.py:run_composability_test`
(α=0.005-0.006, λ=0.2 — tuned for CO/NO+H2+ balance, not these systems)

| System | Fisher MAE | MLR MAE | ΔBIC | Verdict |
|--------|-----------:|--------:|-----:|---------|
| H2+    | 30.9 mHa   | 51.7 mHa | -10.0 | Fisher > MLR |
| H2     | 40.8 mHa   | 55.6 mHa | -5.6  | Fisher > MLR |

But: at the regimes where each framework should shine, MLR did win:
- **H2+ at R=0.7 Å**: MLR error 0.003 Ha vs Fisher 0.026 Ha
- **H2 at R=3.0 Å**: MLR error 0.016 Ha vs Fisher 0.081 Ha (long-bond multireference regime)

The single-calibration-point bias was hiding genuine structure.

### v3 — per-system optimal calibration (in-sample)
**File:** `mlr_vs_fisher_calibrated.py`
**Reference:** same (UHF for H2+, CCSD(T) for H2)
**Calibration:** grid-search α (Fisher) and (α, λ) jointly (MLR), minimize MAE

| System | Best Fisher (α) | Fisher MAE | Best MLR (α, λ) | MLR MAE | ΔBIC | Verdict |
|--------|----------------:|-----------:|----------------:|--------:|-----:|---------|
| H2+    | α=0.012         | 26.7 mHa   | α=0.03, λ=0.2   | 26.1 mHa | -4.6  | MLR ≈ Fisher |
| H2     | α=0.001         | 24.5 mHa   | α=0.002, λ=0.05 | **16.7 mHa** | **+3.9** | **MLR > Fisher** *(in-sample only; reversed on G2-1 LOOCV — see update)* |

## What this means

**[Superseded — see 2026-04-18 update]**

**For H2 (closed shell, multireference at long bond):** MLR beats Fisher by 32%
in MAE *and* survives the BIC penalty for its extra parameter. ΔBIC = +3.9 is
"positive evidence for MLR" by Kass-Raftery scale (2-6 = positive). The energy
term is doing real work in the regime where DFT's classical failure modes
(left-right correlation, stretched-bond multireference) are most prominent.

**For H2+ (one electron, SIE-dominated):** MLR and Fisher are essentially tied.
The MAE improvement is tiny (0.6 mHa) and BIC penalizes MLR's extra parameter.
This is a genuine null — neither framework dominates here.

**[Superseded — see 2026-04-18 update]** **For the broader MLR vs Fisher
claim:** The energy term earns its place on at least one canonical chemistry
benchmark. The original "MLR with E term beats Fisher without it" hypothesis
is not refuted; it has support on H2 and is inconclusive on H2+. ~~This is a
real, publishable empirical result~~ — the first direct head-to-head between
the two frameworks on shared data *(reversed out-of-sample; see update)*.

## What this is NOT

- **Not an out-of-sample test.** Calibration and evaluation used the same 7-8
  radii. With so few points, no clean train/test split is possible. Results
  represent each model's *best fit* to this data, not predictive accuracy on
  unseen geometries.
- **Not a generalization claim.** Two systems (H2, H2+), one basis (def2-svp),
  one base functional (PBE). Larger benchmarks (G2, BH76, MR-MGM-BE4) are
  needed before claiming this transfers.
- **Not a falsification of Fisher.** Fisher information field theory is alive
  and well; this only shows that adding an energy channel improves fit on H2.

## Honest caveats

1. **Calibration sensitivity is huge.** Default calibration (v2) made MLR look
   bad. Optimal calibration (v3) made it look good. The right number is
   somewhere between — out-of-sample testing on unseen molecules will pull MAE
   numbers up.
2. **The H2+ tie is the more reliable result** because UHF is the exact
   reference for one electron. The H2 win depends on CCSD(T) being a clean
   truth proxy, which is essentially true for def2-svp but not perfect.
3. **SCC v1 vs v2 history matters.** The original `composability_h2.csv` /
   `composability_h2plus.csv` files use v1 and should be regenerated with v2 if
   they're cited elsewhere. The 2% composability claim was tested on v1.

## What to run next (priority order)

1. **Larger benchmark with proper LOOCV.** G2-1 set (~50 molecules, atomization
   energies), per-molecule LOOCV calibration, report MAE on held-out molecules.
2. **Other base functionals.** Repeat with B3LYP, BLYP, M06 to test whether
   MLR's advantage is base-functional-independent.
3. **Stretched-bond benchmark.** BH76 or MR-MGM-BE4 — these are designed to
   stress test multireference handling, where MLR's win on H2 should
   generalize.
4. **Per-region SCC tuning.** ρ_s and h_power were not optimized here. They
   could probably reduce MLR MAE further on H2+.

## 2026-04-18 update: G2-1 LOOCV done, λ-bridge gap exposed

### G2-1 LOOCV result
Ran `mlr_vs_fisher_g2_loocv.py` on a 12-molecule G2-1 subset (H2, LiH, CH4, NH3,
H2O, HF, N2, F2, CO, HCl, H2S, LiF) with 8 atoms (H, Li, C, N, O, F, S, Cl) and
a 4×4 (α, λ_SCC) grid. Reference: CCSD(T)/def2-svp atomization energies.

| Metric | Fisher | MLR | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Mean MAE (out-of-sample) | 25.7 mHa | 58.1 mHa | Fisher 2.3× better |
| Wins on held-out molecule | 12/12 | 0/12 | Fisher swept |
| ΔBIC | — | — | −20.3 (very strong evidence for Fisher) |

The H2 in-sample win (32% MAE reduction) **did not generalize**. Two reasons:
(1) atomization energy compounds errors from atoms AND molecules in a way
total energy does not, (2) in-sample optimization on 7 points was essentially
fitting noise.

### Diagnostic: SCC over-binds molecules vs atoms
`diagnose_scc_ae.py` decomposed the AE error per molecule:

| Mol | sum(ΔE_atom from SCC) | ΔE_mol from SCC | ΔAE_SCC |
|-----|---:|---:|---:|
| H2 | -18.2 | -22.5 | **+4.4** |
| CH4 | -198.8 | -254.5 | **+55.7** |
| NH3 | -191.3 | -248.0 | **+56.7** |
| H2S | -878.6 | -956.3 | **+77.8** |

Sign positive every time — molecule shifts more than sum of atoms. The SCC
gate `w_eff = w(z) × h(ρ)` activates in low-density single-orbital regions;
bonding regions are exactly such regions, so bonding electrons get an extra
dose of self-Coulomb removal that core atomic electrons don't. The mechanism
is interpretable, not random.

### λ-bridge gap (the real finding)
The G2-1 LOOCV used λ_SCC (dimensionless rescaling in `SCCModule`) as a
stand-in for MLR's λ_E (action coefficient on the energy term in
𝓡 = K + H + λE). **There is no derivation linking these two quantities.**
The wiki's [λ Convention Audit](https://github.com/curv-institute/curv-wiki/blob/main/wiki/topics/fix-lambda-convention-audit.md)
flags exactly this kind of substitution as forbidden:

> "Until equivalence is proven, use provisional names: λ_E for the multiplier
> in 𝓡 = K + H + λE… Deprecate naked λ in public docs until all mappings are
> explicit."

### K19 scaling test confirms the gap empirically
Ran `k19_scaling_test.py`. K19 claims a universal scaling law S ∝ λ⁻¹ with
R² = 1.000 across the MLR optimizer/A2/cosmology domains. We tested whether
λ_SCC obeys this law on our chemistry data:

- Mean R² = **0.871** across 20 entities (8 atoms + 12 molecules)
- 0/20 reach K19's claimed R² = 1.000
- Slopes consistently positive (S decreases as λ_SCC increases) — the
  relationship is real, but not pure 1/λ
- λ_SCC is **structurally not a member** of the K19/optimizer/A2 λ family

### Reframing
Combined with the dimensional analysis in
[`lambda-catalog.md`](https://github.com/curv-institute/curv-wiki/blob/main/wiki/topics/lambda-catalog.md):

- λ_SCC is dimensionless O(0.1)
- λ_E is 1/Energy
- λ_SIRS = 1/(k_B T ln 2) ≈ 1530/Ha
- K19's λ obeys S ∝ λ⁻¹; λ_SCC does not *(K19's S ∝ λ⁻¹ was later found to be a definitional identity, not an empirical law — withdrawn 2026-04-18)*

**The G2-1 LOOCV result is best read as "SCC v2 hurts atomization energies in
LOOCV" — a real and useful finding about the SCC operationalization, but NOT
a test of MLR theory.** A defensible MLR vs Fisher chemistry test requires
either (a) a derivation showing λ_SCC = c · λ_E in this normalization, or
(b) a new operationalization of E as an explicit information-cost term
closer to λ_SIRS's reading.
